The QA Skills That Become MORE Valuable as AI Takes Over

RN
5 min read
2025

Everyone's worried AI is coming for QA jobs. It is. Specifically, it's coming for the part where you click the same 47 buttons every regression cycle. Good riddance.

The rest of the job? Still very much a human problem. And the engineers who thrive? They're not hiding from AI — they're the ones who figured out how to make it work harder than anyone else on the team.

🧠 Testing what isn't written

AI generates test cases from what exists. It is completely blind to what's missing. The undocumented workflow. The assumption nobody wrote down because it felt obvious. The edge case that only matters if you understand why the feature exists.

Where AI helps: Prompt it with the spec and the context — the upstream system, the data shape, the user type. You get 80% coverage in minutes. Your job is the remaining 20% that matters most.

🌍 Reading the room

Requirements are written by people who already know the answer — which means they skip the parts that feel obvious to them. AI reads the document. It cannot read the room. That's the job.

Where AI helps: Feed it the requirements and ask "what scenarios are missing here?" Then apply your judgment to what it surfaces. Machine speed, human judgment — that combination is the actual skill.

🔍 Questioning the definition, not just the feature

Anyone can verify a feature does what the ticket says. The harder question: should it do that? AI optimises for coverage of what's defined. It doesn't challenge the definition. The person asking "but what about..." when everyone else is mentally at lunch? Indispensable. Increasingly rare. Not replaceable.

Where AI helps: Generate the happy path automatically. Free yourself up to spend time on the flows nobody drew — the unhappy paths, the real-user paths, the "what if the data is dirty" paths.

⚙️ Automation that actually means something

AI can generate automation scripts. It will also confidently automate the wrong thing if you let it. Knowing what to automate — and what not to — is a skill that just became more valuable, not less. Stable, meaningful, maintainable tests don't come from a prompt. They come from understanding the system well enough to know what's worth locking down.

Where AI helps: Scaffolding, boilerplate, selector generation, test data setup. Let it do the grunt work. You own the strategy. A poorly directed AI produces fast, green, meaningless tests. Sound familiar? We've been fighting that problem long before AI showed up.

🤢 The feeling that something's off

Automation green. Coverage solid. AI signed off. And yet.

That feeling is pattern recognition — not anxiety. AI has no memory of the last time everything looked fine right before a P1 on a Friday at 4:47pm. You do. Trust it.

Bottom Line

The engineers who thrive aren't the ones who know the most tools — or the ones who avoid them. They're the ones who use AI to their advantage, know what to question, and know what to hand off.
