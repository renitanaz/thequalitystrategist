21 JAWS on Windows: Enterprise Screen Reader Testing

JAWS is the most widely used screen reader in enterprise environments. If your product ships to corporate clients, JAWS testing is not optional.

1. JAWS vs NVDA: When to Use Which

NVDA is free and representative of a large proportion of screen reader users, making it the default for most accessibility testing. JAWS is the correct choice when your product targets enterprise users, government agencies, corporate employees, or regulated industries where JAWS is the deployed standard.

WebAIM's Screen Reader User Survey consistently places JAWS as the most used screen reader globally by a significant margin among professional users. NVDA is more common among home users and in accessibility testing programmes due to its zero cost.

Test with NVDA first to catch the majority of issues. Add JAWS testing when the product targets enterprise environments, when a JAWS-specific defect has been reported, or when the testing programme requires validated compatibility with both screen readers.

2. Setup and Licensing

  • Licence: JAWS is paid software by Freedom Scientific. Annual licence approximately $95/year for home use, higher for professional licences. Enterprise licences are site-based.
  • Download: support.freedomscientific.com/Downloads/JAWS
  • 40-minute mode: JAWS can be run without a licence for 40 minutes per session before requiring restart. Sufficient for focused testing sessions.
  • JAWS key: Insert (same as NVDA by default). Can be reconfigured to Caps Lock.
  • Recommended browser: Chrome for modern web apps. JAWS also has strong IE/Edge support for legacy enterprise applications.
  • Launch: From the Start menu or desktop shortcut. JAWS announces "JAWS for Windows" on start.
  • Quit: Insert+F4
  • Silence speech: Ctrl

4. Virtual Cursor and Forms Mode

JAWS uses a virtual cursor (equivalent to NVDA's browse mode) and forms mode. The behaviour is nearly identical to NVDA:

  • Virtual cursor: default mode for web pages. Arrow keys navigate content. Single letter shortcuts (H, U, B, F) work.
  • Forms mode: activates when focus enters a form field or interactive widget. Single letter shortcuts stop working. Arrow keys operate the component.
  • Toggle: Insert+Z: JAWS announces "Virtual PC Cursor off" (forms mode) or "Virtual PC Cursor on" (reading mode).
  • Auto-switching: JAWS switches modes automatically on Tab to a form field. If it does not, a widget is failing to set the correct ARIA role or application mode.

5. What to Test and What to Listen For

Page structure

Use Insert+F6 for the headings list: shows the complete heading outline. Use Insert+F7 for the links list: any "click here" or "read more" entries are immediately visible as failures.

Forms

Tab to each field. JAWS announces label, type, and required state. For radio groups: the group legend must be announced before the first option. For custom dropdowns: JAWS must announce the role (combobox or listbox), the selected value, and the expanded/collapsed state.

Correct field announcement: "Email address, required, edit text"
Correct custom dropdown: "Country, collapsed, combobox"
Failure: "edit text" with nothing else: label missing

Dynamic content

JAWS handles role="alert" and aria-live regions. Trigger dynamic updates while focus is elsewhere. JAWS must announce updates from role="status" politely and role="alert" immediately.

PDF and Office documents

JAWS has strong support for accessible PDFs and Microsoft Office documents: areas where NVDA is weaker. If the product includes document export or PDF viewing, JAWS testing is particularly important for those flows.

When to prioritise JAWS testing
  • Product ships to enterprise, government, or regulated industry clients
  • A defect has been reported specifically on JAWS that does not reproduce on NVDA
  • Testing complex ARIA widgets (data grids, tree views, tabs) where JAWS behaviour may differ
  • Testing PDF or Office document accessibility
