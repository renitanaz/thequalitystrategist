CCAR-F for QA: what the blueprint actually asks of you

The Architect Foundations blueprint, read by someone whose job is to break things, not ship them.

The Quality Strategist · Capsule hub · 6 min read

There is heavy CCAR-F prep content out there. There is heavy "AI for testers" content too. Almost nothing connects the two.

Every prep resource explains the Architect Foundations blueprint for people shipping features: build the agent, wire the tool, ship the workflow. Nobody explains what the same blueprint means when your job is the opposite: break the agent, find where the tool fails silently, refuse to sign off on the workflow until it's proven itself. That gap is the entire reason this capsule exists.

I've registered for CCAR-F. I have not sat it yet, no exam date scheduled. Everything here is a QA architect's working read of the public blueprint, cross-checked against the sources at the bottom of this page, not a credential-holder's retrospective. That distinction matters more here than almost anywhere else on this site: writing confidently about an exam you haven't sat is exactly the kind of unverified claim this whole site exists to push back against. I'll update this line the day that changes.

The registration gate

Stated early because it changes whether this capsule is even relevant to you: registration requires a work email on a domain tied to a Claude Partner Network organisation. A personal email won't get you in. If you're not sure your employer is on that list, that's worth confirming before you invest time in the rest of this capsule, not after.

Who should not bother with this

This is the credibility section, so it's deliberately not softened.

  • You're prepping to build and ship AI features, not test them. Go read the vendor blueprint walkthroughs instead, they're written for that job, this capsule isn't.
  • You want exam dumps, brain dumps, or answer keys. This capsule doesn't have them and wouldn't publish them if it did.
  • You've never used Claude or Claude Code in any testing capacity. The QA translations here assume you've at least tried the base tools; without that, the framing will read as abstract rather than useful.
  • You need this today. Four of the five domain posts don't exist yet. This capsule publishes one post at a time, interleaved with everything else on the site, not on a fixed schedule.

What this capsule actually is

Five posts, five working deliverables, all built against PeakAndPack, the same demo app the Playwright and AI-Augmented QA series already use. Every post: one concept, one worked example, a boxed line tying it to the exact blueprint domain and weight, and a closing section naming what it doesn't solve. Under 8 minutes to read, on purpose, the competition here is 3,000-word vendor walkthroughs written by people who have never filed a bug. Density from a quality seat is the actual differentiator.

This capsule sits outside the site's main series numbering and does not change the build order of anything else. It publishes as one interleaved post at a time between other work, never as a parallel track.
