The QA Report, Written By Hand

Scope, approach, results, risk assessment, and recommendation — in one document a stakeholder can act on.

By RN ~5 min read Series: AI-Augmented QA
Manual, No AI Manual + AI Automated, Playwright AI QA Agent

A QA summary report is the document that answers: what was tested, what was found, what the risk picture looks like, and what should happen next. Writing it by hand, without AI, forces you to synthesise everything you know about the system into one coherent view.

What a good QA summary report contains. Five sections: Scope (what was in and out of scope), Approach (how it was tested), Results (what was found), Risk assessment (what remains risky after the findings), and Recommendation (release, release with conditions, or do not release, with justification). A report missing the recommendation is incomplete.

PeakAndPack QA Summary Report

Scope

All six PeakAndPack feature areas tested: products, search, cart, authentication, orders, and checkout. Testing covered functional, visual, API contract, and end-to-end concerns across eight phases.

Out of scope: performance testing, security penetration testing, third-party payment provider integration, and browser compatibility beyond Chromium.

Approach

Four approaches were applied across all phases, providing multiple perspectives on the same system:

ApproachUsed in phasesWhat it contributed
Manual, No AIAll phasesGround truth. Human judgment on what matters. Baseline for every other approach.
Manual + AIAll phasesSpeed on first drafts. Surfaced gaps the manual pass missed (BUG-013 enumeration risk, BUG-014 split).
Automated, PlaywrightPhases 3, 4, 5, 6, 7Repeatable regression coverage. Visual baseline comparison. CI-ready bug detection.
AI QA AgentPhases 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7Scale at low effort. Strategy reasoning, test case generation, visual review, flow analysis, bug write-up.

Seven bugs found across the full test programme. Two Critical, three High, one Medium, one Low.

Risk assessment

Two findings carry risk that makes release without fix inadvisable. BUG-009 means every checkout using SAVE10 results in $0 revenue — at any transaction volume, this is a direct and ongoing financial loss. BUG-013 is a data privacy violation: any authenticated user can read any other user's order history, and a malicious caller could enumerate all customer purchases by iterating user IDs.

The remaining five bugs range from High (BUG-008, 011, 014) to Low (BUG-012). All are important but none carries the combination of likelihood and business impact of the two Critical findings.

Recommendation

Do not release to production until BUG-009 and BUG-013 are fixed and verified. Both are Critical severity with direct revenue and privacy consequences.

BUG-008, BUG-011, and BUG-014 should be fixed in the same release cycle. BUG-010 and BUG-012 can follow in a subsequent release given their lower severity and limited user impact.

Verification: the automated test suite in checkout.spec.ts, api.spec.ts, and e2e-flows.spec.ts contains tests designed to pass once each bug is fixed. No manual re-test is needed — run the suite, confirm the previously-failing tests turn green.
