Phase 8 of 8 · Reporting

The QA Report, Synthesised by an Agent

Feed it every deliverable from the series. Get one complete QA programme summary with a clear release recommendation.

By RN ~5 min read Series: AI-Augmented QA
Manual, No AI Manual + AI Automated, Playwright AI QA Agent

The reporting agent reads every deliverable produced across the eight phases and synthesises them into one complete QA programme summary. It does what the manual approach does, but in minutes rather than an hour, and across a volume of input that would take a human significant time to read and structure.

What it does

  1. You give it the key deliverables from all phases: the test strategy, the test cases, the visual checklist, the API contract results, the E2E flow results, and the bug detection report.
  2. It reads across all of them and produces: a programme summary, a full bug list with severities, a phase-by-phase approach comparison, and a release recommendation.
  3. It flags anything it could not assess from the provided deliverables alone.

The system prompt

You are a QA programme analyst. You will be given the deliverables
from an eight-phase QA programme for PeakAndPack, a trekking and
travel gear e-commerce app.

Produce a complete QA programme summary report containing:

1. Programme scope: what was and was not tested.
2. Approach summary: the four approaches used and what each contributed.
3. Full bug list: all bugs found, with ID, title, severity, and
   the phase that first surfaced each.
4. Risk assessment: which findings block release and why, in
   business language a non-technical stakeholder can act on.
5. Release recommendation: a clear verdict (release / release with
   conditions / do not release) with the specific conditions if any.
6. Approach comparison: which approach caught what, what each approach
   costs in time, and what each contributes that the others cannot.

Rules:
- Base every finding on the deliverables provided. Do not invent bugs
  or findings not in the input.
- Use business language in the risk assessment and recommendation.
  Translate technical issues into user and business impact.
- The recommendation must be a clear verdict. Do not hedge.
- If a finding has a severity, use it. Do not reassign severities
  without explaining why.
- Return only the report. No preamble.

What the series taught

The answer to "which approach is best" is the wrong question. Every approach in the series found something the others either missed or would have been slower to find. Manual found the right things to test. AI-assisted wrote them up faster and caught gaps. Automated turns the findings into a regression suite that runs forever. Agents scale the high-volume, structured parts so human effort goes to judgment.

The combination is the point. A QA programme that uses all four approaches is more than four times as effective as one that uses only one.

Your deliverable

One file: qa-programme-summary.md, the complete QA programme summary for PeakAndPack. Review every section, confirm the recommendation matches your own assessment, and replace any technical jargon with business language before sharing with a stakeholder.

Time: ~10 minutes to generate. The review is the work.
