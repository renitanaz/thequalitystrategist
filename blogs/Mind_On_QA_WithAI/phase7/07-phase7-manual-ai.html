Bug Detection, With a Second Opinion

Provide the evidence. Claude drafts the report. You review, correct, and confirm before anything is filed.

By RN ~5 min read Series: AI-Augmented QA
Manual, No AI Manual + AI Automated, Playwright AI QA Agent

Same seven confirmed bugs, but this time the heavy lifting of the write-up goes to Claude. You provide the evidence — a screenshot, an API response, a failing test output — and Claude drafts the structured bug report. Your job is to review, correct severity, and confirm the steps actually reproduce the issue before anything is filed. This pass also produced one false positive that the review step should have caught, see below.

The prompt that works. Paste the evidence and say: "This is the output of a test/screenshot/API call for PeakAndPack. Write a structured bug report with: Steps to reproduce, Expected, Actual, Severity (Critical/High/Medium/Low with justification), and Evidence. Base severity on user and business impact, not just technical severity."

The review rule that applies here

AI bug reports sound authoritative whether or not they are accurate. The one thing to always check: do the steps to reproduce actually reproduce the issue? Claude writes plausible steps from the evidence it sees. If the evidence is a screenshot, it cannot know exactly which user account or data state produced it. Read the steps and ask whether a developer following them will see the same bug. If not, fix the steps before filing.

Severity is yours, not the AI's. Claude will reason about severity from the description, and it is usually in the right ballpark. But severity has business context the AI does not have — a "Low" cosmetic bug on the checkout page might be "High" for a specific client. Always confirm severity against your own understanding of the business, not just the AI's reasoning.
