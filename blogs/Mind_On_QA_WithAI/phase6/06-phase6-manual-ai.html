Phase 6 of 8 · E2E Flows

E2E Flows, With a Coverage Check

Your notes go in loose. Formal scenarios, and at least one flow you hadn't thought of, come out.

By RN ~4 min read Series: AI-Augmented QA
Manual, No AI Manual + AI Automated, Playwright AI QA Agent

Document the manual run, then hand the results to Claude to find gaps, formalize loose notes into real test scenarios, and surface flows you didn't think to test.

What you actually do

  • Document what you found in the manual flows, even if loosely
  • Ask Claude to identify gaps in your flow coverage
  • Ask Claude to write up formal test scenarios from your notes
  • Ask Claude to suggest additional edge case flows you might not have considered

Prompts that actually work

I tested this checkout flow and got these results. What did I miss? [paste results]

What are the most important E2E flows for an e-commerce app that I should be testing?

Convert these informal notes into formal E2E test scenarios with pass/fail criteria. [paste notes]

What could go wrong between the cart and checkout steps that I haven't tested?

A gap this approach actually caught

Concurrent cart modification. The 5 manual flows tested cart operations one at a time. Asking Claude what could go wrong between cart and checkout surfaced a flow none of the 5 originally covered: what happens if a second tab modifies the same cart while checkout is in progress. Not one of the 11 known bugs, but a legitimate gap in flow coverage that pure manual testing missed.

Your deliverable

One file: e2e-flows-ai-assisted.md, the formalized scenarios plus any newly identified flows, each with clear pass/fail criteria.

Time: ~45 minutes. The speed gain here is specifically in formalizing loose notes into something with real pass/fail criteria, not in discovering the flows themselves.
