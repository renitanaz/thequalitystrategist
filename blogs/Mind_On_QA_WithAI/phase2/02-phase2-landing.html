Test Strategy: Four Ways to Decide What Matters

Before any test case gets written, someone has to decide what's worth testing. Here's that decision made four different ways.

By RN ~4 min read Series: AI-Augmented QA

A confession heard on every team: “I'll just write test cases, why do I need a strategy first?”

Because without one, you test whatever's easiest to click, not what's riskiest to break. Test strategy is the thinking layer: what to test, what to skip, and why, before a single test case gets written.

What "strategy" actually means here

Not test cases. Not scripts. The decisions that come before those: which features matter most, what's in scope, what's explicitly out, and what counts as "good enough to ship."

Risk first, not bugs first. Before writing a single test case, rank PeakAndPack's known risk areas (checkout, auth, pricing) by business impact, not by which bug is easiest to find. A $0 checkout discount is worse than a missing product image, even if the image bug is more obvious.

Same goal, four different roads

Every approach below answers the same question, who decides what to test and how, but the actor changes: you alone, you with AI assistance, a config file enforcing rules, or an agent generating the whole thing.
