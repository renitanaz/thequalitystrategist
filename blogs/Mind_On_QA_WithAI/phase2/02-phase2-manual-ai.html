Phase 2 of 8 · Test Strategy

Test Strategy, With a Second Opinion

You still write the strategy. Claude just makes sure you're not missing something obvious.

By RN ~4 min read Series: AI-Augmented QA
Test Strategy: Manual, No AI Test Strategy: Manual + AI Test Strategy: Automated, Playwright Test Strategy: AI QA Agent

Same goal as the manual approach, decide what's risky and write a strategy, but now Claude pressure-tests your thinking before you commit it to the document.

What you actually do

  1. Share peakandpack-requirements.md with Claude
  2. Ask Claude to identify risks, suggest test types, and challenge your draft
  3. You write the final strategy. Claude accelerates the thinking, it doesn't replace the decision

Prompts that actually work

Here are the PeakAndPack requirements. What are the top 5 riskiest areas to test and why?

What test types would you recommend for a trekking gear checkout flow with a discount code?

What am I missing from this test strategy? [paste your draft]

Risk-based prioritisation. At this stage you don't know where the bugs are yet, that's Phase 7's job. What you can rank now is risk by feature area: which parts of PeakAndPack would cause the most damage if something went wrong there, known bug or not. Ask Claude: “Given PeakAndPack's feature areas (products, cart, checkout, auth, orders), rank them by business risk if something broke there, which would cause the most damage in production?” This shifts effort toward analysis up front instead of waiting to find problems by accident.

Where this beats pure manual

Manual alone
You only catch risks you already know to look for. Blind spots stay blind.
Manual + AI
Claude surfaces risk categories you didn't think to ask about (e.g. SQL injection in sort params), then you decide if they're worth including.

Your deliverable

One file: test-strategy-ai-assisted.md. Same structure as the manual version, scope, priorities, entry/exit criteria, but stress-tested against a second opinion before it's final.

Time: ~45 minutes. The thinking is faster, not because it's shallower, but because Claude does the first pass of "what am I missing" instead of you doing it alone.
