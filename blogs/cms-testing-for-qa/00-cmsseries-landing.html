CMS Testing for QA

Most websites aren't hand-coded, they're assembled from a content management system by people who aren't developers, then extended with plugins, themes, and third-party integrations nobody on the QA team chose. That changes what "testing the website" actually has to cover. This series builds a complete, platform-agnostic testing strategy, mapped across the SDLC, so by the end you know exactly what to check and why, not just how to click through one tool.

By RN 17 posts Demos: self-built site, WordPress Playground, Strapi Prerequisite: None

Why CMS testing needs its own strategy

A CMS splits a website into two layers that change on different schedules, by different people: the code a developer ships deliberately, and the content an editor publishes independently, often daily, with no code review or deploy pipeline in between.

Most test plans are written for the first layer and quietly forget the second exists, until a non-technical edit breaks SEO, accessibility, or a page layout in production with nobody watching.

Every step comes with a reason. This series doesn't just list what to check for SEO, accessibility, or performance. It explains why that check matters for a CMS-driven site specifically, what breaks in production if it's skipped, and where in the SDLC it needs to happen to actually catch something. The goal is a strategy you can apply to any CMS you're handed, not a checklist for one specific tool.

The environments this series uses

There's no single demo app here the way PeakAndPack anchors the Playwright and Proxyman series, because "CMS testing" spans risks that no single CMS demonstrates on its own. Instead, this series uses three free environments, each picked for what it can actually show:

A self-built demo site

A small static site, "Grove & Bean," seeded deliberately with real issues: missing alt text, no meta tags, unoptimized images, a contact form, analytics gaps. Used for every post where the risk is true of any website regardless of what generates it: SEO, accessibility, performance, analytics, cross-browser, forms, legal. Open the demo site → (full answer key in demo-site/seeded-issues.md).

WordPress Playground

A full, real WordPress site that runs entirely in the browser, no install, no hosting, live in seconds, at wordpress.org/playground. Used for editorial workflow, plugin and theme risk, and everything that's genuinely specific to a classic CMS, since WordPress alone runs roughly 42% of all websites.

Strapi Community

A free, self-hosted, open-source headless CMS. Used for the API and headless-specific posts, where the thing under test is a content API rather than a rendered page.

Automated tools only catch part of the picture. Lighthouse, axe, and similar scanners typically catch around 30-40% of real accessibility issues, and free SEO crawlers catch roughly two-thirds of what a full paid audit finds. They're the right starting point in every post here, not the full stop, manual verification gets called out wherever it matters.

Who this is for

  • QA engineers testing a marketing site, blog, or e-commerce storefront built on any CMS, WordPress, Shopify, a headless setup, or something in-house.
  • Anyone whose test plan currently covers "does the page load" but not SEO, accessibility, analytics, or what happens when a non-technical editor publishes something unexpected.
  • Testers who want one coherent strategy that applies across CMS platforms, not a tutorial tied to one product.

No prerequisite series, though the API basics primer from the AI-Augmented QA series will help for posts 4 and 5.
