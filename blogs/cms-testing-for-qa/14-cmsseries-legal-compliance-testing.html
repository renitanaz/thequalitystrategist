Legal and compliance testing

Adding a tracking script to a CMS is a one-line change with no compliance review attached to it, the same content/code-layer gap from Post 1, now with legal exposure instead of a display bug. Nobody has to approve a script tag before it goes live.

By RN ~9 min read Demo: Grove & Bean

Key takeaways
  • A cookie banner that appears while tracking scripts already loaded underneath it isn't compliant, consent has to happen before the script runs, not alongside it.
  • A Privacy Policy that exists but isn't linked from anywhere a visitor would actually find it functionally doesn't exist for compliance purposes.
  • Accessibility failures (Post 7) are increasingly a legal exposure category on their own, not just a UX concern.
  • None of this requires a lawyer to test the basics: does a script load before consent, is the policy reachable, both are checkable in a browser.

Consent has to gate the script, not just appear near it

A correct consent implementation blocks non-essential scripts, analytics, ad pixels, marketing tools, from loading until the visitor actively agrees. A common broken implementation shows a consent banner while the exact same scripts load immediately underneath it, regardless of what's clicked.

Why "we have a cookie banner" isn't the actual check

A banner's presence is easy to verify and easy to add without changing anything about when scripts actually load. The real check is behavioral: open DevTools, watch the Network tab on first page load, and see whether the analytics request fires before any consent choice was made. If it does, the banner is decorative, not functional.

Privacy policy: existing isn't the same as reachable

A Privacy Policy page that exists at some URL, but isn't linked from the footer, navigation, or anywhere a visitor would naturally find it, doesn't meaningfully satisfy the purpose of having one. Testing this is a straightforward reachability check, not a legal judgment call.

Grove & Bean's footer only links to Contact. There's no Privacy Policy link anywhere on the site (LEGAL-2), on top of the unconditional analytics script (LEGAL-1). Both are simple, verifiable, and both are the kind of gap that CMS content changes reintroduce easily if a footer template is edited without anyone checking what got removed.

Accessibility as a compliance category, not just a UX one

Post 7 covered accessibility as a usability concern. It's worth naming directly here too: accessibility lawsuits, based on WCAG conformance, are a real and growing legal exposure category in several jurisdictions. The checks from Post 7 aren't purely a UX nicety, they're part of this post's territory as much as Post 7's.

Try it: verify both gaps on the demo site

Try it

Open the Grove & Bean homepage with DevTools → Network open, filtered to collect or gtag. Reload the page and check the timestamp of the first analytics request against page load, and note whether any consent banner ever appeared.

Then check the footer on every page for a Privacy Policy link. Note whether one exists anywhere on the site.

Check your findings against demo-site/seeded-issues.md, LEGAL-1 and LEGAL-2.

Your deliverable

A short note: whether the analytics script fired before, during, or with no relation at all to any consent mechanism, and one sentence on what a real visitor from a jurisdiction with consent requirements would actually experience.

Time: ~15 minutes.
