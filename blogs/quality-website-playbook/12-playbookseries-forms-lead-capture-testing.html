Forms and lead-capture testing

A contact form is usually the one place on a CMS site where a stranger's data goes somewhere real: an inbox, a CRM, a spreadsheet. It's also frequently built with a separate plugin or form-builder tool, bolted onto the CMS rather than native to it, with its own validation logic, or none at all.

Key takeaways
  • Forms are often built with a separate plugin or builder tool, with its own validation logic, sometimes none at all, disconnected from the CMS's usual patterns.
  • A form that visually "submits" without an error is not proof the data went anywhere. Test what happens after the click, not just whether a click is possible.
  • Validation, spam handling, delivery, and error states are four separate checks. A form can pass three and silently fail the fourth.
  • The most dangerous form bug is invisible: it looks successful to the visitor and delivers nothing to anyone.

The four things a form actually has to get right

CheckWhat it catches
ValidationRequired fields enforced, email format checked, before submission is accepted
Spam/bot handlingA honeypot field, CAPTCHA, or rate limit keeps automated submissions from flooding whoever receives them
DeliveryA valid submission actually reaches its destination: an inbox, a CRM, a database
Error statesA failed submission (network issue, server error, spam block) tells the visitor something went wrong, instead of pretending success

The most dangerous failure looks like success

A form with no real backend, or a broken connection to one, can still show a "thank you" message or simply do nothing alarming when clicked. Visually, that's indistinguishable from a form that worked. The visitor walks away assuming their message was sent.

Why this is exactly what happened on the demo site

Grove & Bean's contact form has action="#", pointing nowhere. Filling it in and clicking Send does nothing visible: no error, no success message, no page change. A visitor has no way to know whether their message went anywhere, and neither would a tester who only checked that the button was clickable.

Try it: run all four checks on the demo site

Try it

Open the Grove & Bean contact form. Try submitting it completely empty. Note whether anything stops you.

Enter not-an-email in the email field and submit. Note whether the format is validated before submission is accepted.

Finally, fill in every field correctly and submit. Watch closely for any success message, error message, or visible page change. Note what actually happens, not what you'd expect to happen.

Check your findings against demo-site/seeded-issues.md, FORMS-1 through FORMS-3.

Your deliverable

A short bug report for the contact form, written the way you'd file it for real: steps to reproduce, expected behavior, actual behavior, for whichever of the three checks above you found broken.

Time: ~15 minutes.
